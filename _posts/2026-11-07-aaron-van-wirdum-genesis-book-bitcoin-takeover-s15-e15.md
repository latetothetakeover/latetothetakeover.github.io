I thought Bitcoin just *happened* one day in 2008. Aaron van Wirdum spent six years proving that take is cooked.

**How late am I? ⏰** Aired January 12, 2024. I was still in high school arguing whether Bitcoin was "just a stock." Late by about two years and ten months.

## The book that refuses the alien-Satoshi lore

Vlad sits down with Aaron about *The Genesis Book*: Bitcoin's pre-history. Aaron started the research in early 2018, first as magazine pieces, then kept grinding until the paperback landed. He open-sourced the manuscript at Bitcoin 2023 in Miami because the book itself argues free and open source tends to make better products. Writing a closed book about that felt wrong. His bet: if people like it, they'll want a physical copy; if they can't afford it, he'd rather they read it free than not at all. Chat joked about refunding Amazon. Aaron's counter: you can't flex a PDF on the bookshelf behind your webcam. Fair.

Highlights that stuck:

- **E-gold as the cold open.** Douglas Jackson's gold-backed digital tokens worked until the government hammered the company. Aaron compares it to the first scene of *Star Wars*: show the Empire crushing the rebel ship, *then* tell how a different design survived. E-gold dies as Satoshi is already building. Suddenly "why stay pseudonymous?" stops being a conspiracy meme.
- **Foundations → Extropians → Cypherpunks.** Hayek on one track, hacker culture and Chaum's e-cash on the other. They collide with the Extropians. Hal Finney was already pushing digital cash there. Then Tim May, Eric Hughes and John Gilmore turn that into Cypherpunk meetings — tools in the wild, not papers in a drawer.
- **The climb:** Hashcash, Bit Gold, b-money, RPOW. On Bit Gold, Aaron's real unsolved piece wasn't just difficulty — it was double-spend prevention without a trusted club of servers.
- **Satoshi's last move.** The book ends where Bitcoin begins, except Satoshi leaving. Aaron treats that as the final touch. Technically a founder staying wouldn't break decentralization. On a human level, natural authority warps the culture. He won't dox. He does suspect Satoshi appears in the book under a real name. Buy the paperback and play detective.

His through-line: **"Bitcoin didn't just appear out of nothing."**

**Lore Drop 📜:** By 2005 most cypherpunks had given up on digital cash. Hal Finney hadn't — that's when RPOW shipped. Gossip still names him as a Satoshi candidate. The book itself doesn't play that game.
