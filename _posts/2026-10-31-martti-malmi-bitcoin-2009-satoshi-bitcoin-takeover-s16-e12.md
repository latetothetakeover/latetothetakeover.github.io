Two episodes of Season 1 made me want the origin story. So I skipped straight to a guy who literally emailed Satoshi. Speedrun strats.

**How late am I? ⏰** Aired March 12, 2025. I was a freshman figuring out where the good microwave in the dorm was. Late by 1 year, 7 months and change, which is honestly my most on-time post yet.

## Meet Sirius

Vlad introduces Martti Malmi, aka Sirius, as the first Bitcoin developer to join Satoshi back in 2009. They're hanging out on Pizza Day, there are beers involved, and Martti is the most low-key OG imaginable. Short answers, zero ego, deadpan bangers.

His whole reason for getting into it: "I wanted to change the world through technology rather than politics," and money felt like the most impactful thing to change. He was Googling for something like peer-to-peer currency, found a plain little bitcoin.org page, read the white paper and emailed Satoshi to offer help. That's it. That was the onboarding.

## Stuff that broke my brain

- **His first job wasn't even code.** Satoshi asked him to write the FAQ and website text, then he set up the forums. He says their emails have "a lot of exciting discussion about WordPress configurations." The creator of Bitcoin, debugging WordPress. Iconic.
- **The 5,000 BTC for five dollars thing.** He ran an early exchange and confirms he's the one who sold 5,000 bitcoin for five bucks in the first known BTC-to-USD trade. He also says he modeled the exchange's look on The Pirate Bay. Peak 2009.
- **No regrets energy.** He burned through a lot of coins when the price crashed and he was between jobs, but says he doesn't feel like he lost anything. Then, on Pizza Day: "at least I got more than two pizzas." Legend.
- **Satoshi vibes.** He first assumed Satoshi was Japanese but had doubts "because his English was so good." And his best argument against the CIA theory: it was always clear Satoshi wasn't the CIA "when I saw him configuring WordPress with me."

**Lore Drop 📜:** Martti says he's a Mt. Gox creditor and it's "public knowledge" they owe him 10 bitcoin. Also, his email address changed in early 2011 and he has no backups, so his *last* emails with Satoshi are just... gone. That one hurt to hear.

## Side Quest 📚

Last week in my econ history lecture, the professor did a whole slide on "private money experiments that failed." Martti literally described those exact projects as "trust me bro, we have gold in our vaults."

I raised my hand and said the first Bitcoin dev was a uni student who just emailed the anonymous founder and got access to the code. The professor said "and how did that go?" I said "he sold 5,000 for five dollars." The room laughed. I'm counting it as a W for participation anyway.

**Explaining it to Uncle Ray:** imagine the guy who caught the first fish in a lake, sold it for a nickel, and still says the trip was worth it.
