Phil Zimmermann gave me PGP. This week the privacy arc handed me the next level: hiding in a crowd, on purpose.

**How late am I? ⏰** Aired May 1, 2020. I was 14, in lockdown, and my biggest privacy concern was Mom reading my texts. Late by six years and six months.

## CoinJoins, explained like a group project

Nopara73 is the guy who built Wasabi Wallet, and this is a 57-minute deep dive from 2020. Vlad asks him the question I'd been too embarrassed to google: what even is a coinjoin, and how is it different from the sketchy old tumblers?

His answer clicked instantly. A bunch of people build one big transaction together, but it only becomes valid once everyone signs. If you don't see the outputs you're owed, you just don't sign. **"This is the magic of coinjoining, that you cannot lose your money."** Basically a group project where nobody can run off with the grade. Wish my stats group worked like that.

Highlights that stuck:

- **Naming things is hard.** The idea where sender and receiver build a payment together was first called the "sender receiver scheme," which Nopara admits "just sounds lame." It became PayJoin. His rule: every PayJoin is a pay to endpoint transaction, but not every pay to endpoint transaction is a PayJoin.
- **He doesn't pretend coinjoins are invisible.** You can't hide in a crowd without the crowd being identifiable, and he flips it: not wanting everyone to know how much money you have shouldn't make you a criminal. Strange logic, he says. Honestly? Same.
- **The beef.** It's 2020, and Vlad asks about Samourai. Nopara opens with "don't use Samourai," then immediately adds "I'm definitely biased." Spicy, but at least he called his own bias.
- **The art take.** He calls his next mixing project possibly his last big one and says, **"I certainly look at it as a piece of art rather than some job that has to be done."** Five years in, still talking like that. Respect.

He also recommends a mobile wallet whose main job is that hitting send "does not give a heart attack to the user." Low bar, but real.

**Lore Drop 📜:** Satoshi's original client had a pay-to-IP feature where you could pay someone's IP address directly. Pay to endpoint is the grown-up version, generalized so an endpoint could be a web address or an onion address too. Bitcoin has been doing callbacks since day one.
