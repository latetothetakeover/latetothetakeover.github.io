Two posts ago I listened to a guy who emailed Satoshi. Now I've gone one level up: the guy who ran the mailing list Satoshi posted to. The lore keeps getting older and I keep getting younger by comparison.

**How late am I? ⏰** Aired July 22, 2026. I was shelving books at the library and ignoring every podcast on earth. Late by about three and a half months.

## Meet the landlord of the internet's most famous inbox

Vlad introduces Perry E. Metzger as the owner of the cryptography mailing list where Bitcoin was first announced. Perry has been online since 1983, and his flex is somehow also a humble brag: he's friends with people who switched on parts of the network back in 1968, so "I'm kind of a newcomer compared to people like that." Bro, if you're a newcomer, I'm a fetus.

Highlights that lived rent-free in my head:

- **The crossbow theory.** Perry got into crypto through an old paper by Chuck Hamill arguing that cryptography is like the crossbow: suddenly a regular person could take down a knight without decades of training. Privacy as the great equalizer. Hard.
- **Why the list existed at all.** The cypherpunks list was "too noisy," so he made a moderated one for actual protocols instead of endless political fights.
- **The confession.** When Satoshi showed up, Perry says there was no particular reason to think it would beat "the previous 30 or 50" digital cash proposals. On a decentralized currency with no central bank behind it: "I was completely wrong." Respect for just saying it.
- **Quantum reality check.** The world record for cleanly factoring a number with a quantum computer? 21. "The average school child can tell you that it's three times seven." I laughed out loud at my desk.

He's not a maxi either: he thinks proof of work adds costs that never go away, and asks how Bitcoin would even switch signature schemes. His answer: "mostly with bloodshed and screaming and arguments." Accurate, judging by the forks lore I've seen so far.

**Lore Drop 📜:** Perry says he still has what he thinks is one of the oldest active PGP keys on the key servers, older than the PGP 2.0 release, and he leaves it up "for amusement sake." A museum piece, still online.

## Side Quest 📟

Perry said something that called me out directly: if you're under about 20, "you barely remember that there was such a thing as a time before smartphones." He used to wear a pager for work and run to find a pay phone when stuff broke.

So last Sunday I asked Dad if he ever had a pager. He went quiet, walked out to the HVAC van, and came back with one from the glovebox. Dead, obviously. Mom said that thing buzzed during their first anniversary dinner and he left to fix someone's furnace. Jade said "so it's giving ancient Apple Watch." The marriage survived. The pager did not.
