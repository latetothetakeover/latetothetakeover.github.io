Four posts deep into Bitcoin's origin story, I finally met the guy whose software Satoshi was using. Privacy arc: officially unlocked.

**How late am I? ⏰** Aired October 19, 2023. I was a high school junior who thought "encryption" was the lock icon in the browser bar. Late by three years and three weeks.

## The OG who refuses the cypherpunk merch

Short one, about 36 minutes, recorded at the Proof of Work Summit in Prague. Phil grips a handheld mic in a blazer, total professor energy. Vlad basically fanboys for a minute ("I'm awestruck and I'm going to stop talking"), and honestly, same.

What I didn't expect: Phil doesn't claim the cypherpunk crown. He was in Boulder, the scene was in Silicon Valley, and he says it straight: **"I'm not a libertarian. I'm not an anarchist. I'm, you know, I'm a moderate."** On the mailing list fights? "I was mostly silent. I just wrote code." Most relatable thing a legend has ever said.

Highlights that live rent-free now:

- **He went broke shipping PGP.** He missed five mortgage payments writing version one and almost lost the house, so it shipped without the trust model. Version two landed 15 months later.
- **MIT had his back.** MIT published the code on its FTP site, which Phil reads as a message to the feds: if putting this online is a crime, arrest MIT too.
- **The NSA's fear was teaching.** A retired senior NSA cryptographer later told him the agency's worry was that the source code would teach people how to do public key crypto properly. Phil says they were afraid of it "metastasizing. And it did."
- **Hal Finney, but make it wholesome.** Hal volunteered almost immediately and built the trust model for version two. Phil calls him a genius with zero ego problems, and says if anyone makes a Hal movie, Tom Hanks has to play him. Then the gut punch: Tom Hanks keeps getting older and Hal doesn't.

## The part that stung a little

Asked when he first heard about Bitcoin, Phil's answer is peak busy-adult: **"I figured I'd get to it later."** He was deep in secure phone calls. He respects Satoshi a lot (and won't speculate who it is), but he's open about what bugs him now: scams, pump-and-dumps, ransomware and the carbon footprint. Hearing that from the PGP guy hits different than hearing it from Mom's news alerts. I don't fully agree, but respect for not telling a Bitcoin show what it wanted to hear.

He does like Shamir's secret sharing for splitting a key so friends can help you recover it. Noted.

**Lore Drop 📜:** Proof of work was pitched against spam before it secured money. Phil describes Microsoft's Penny Black project, where your mail server solved a hash puzzle before an email got delivered. Botnets killed that dream. Bitcoin pulled it off.
