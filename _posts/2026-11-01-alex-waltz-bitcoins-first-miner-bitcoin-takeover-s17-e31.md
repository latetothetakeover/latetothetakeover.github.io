Last post, a guy who emailed Satoshi. This one asks a nastier question: who was actually *mining* back then? Turns out "everybody knows" is not a source.

**How late am I? ⏰** Aired July 2, 2026. I was pretending to do summer reading. Late by just under four months, which in this blog's timeline is basically live.

## The setup

It's a livestream, Vlad on one side, Alex Waltz on the other, and a chat that's there for a giveaway (pick a number from 1 to 100, win 0.1 Zcash). Vlad mentions they first met back in 2019 at a bar in Berlin.

Alex's project is called First Bitcoin Miner, and the charts on screen come straight from his site. The big question: was Hal Finney really the second miner after Satoshi, or is that just a story we repeat because of the "Running bitcoin" tweet?

## The detective board

- **The block gaps.** Alex charts block times for the first 170 blocks and points at huge holes: 41 minutes, two hours, eight hours, 24 hours. His reaction when he saw them: "I knew immediately this is not a coincidence."
- **The extra nonce.** There's a second counter in early Bitcoin that doesn't reset between blocks, only when the node restarts. So it acts like a little uptime tracker. Then the punchline: "Every time there's a reset of the nonce, there's a big time gap between blocks."
- **The exceptions.** Only two spots break the rule, blocks 37 to 38 and 76 to 77, and they fall in the stretch around block 49, when Hal's own debug log shows exactly two other nodes online: one regular, one running over Tor. Alex's read is that both were Satoshi, since version 0.1 needed at least one other peer before it would produce blocks.
- **The clean lab.** He says that in this tiny window "there's literally almost no noise," and compares it to running a physics experiment in a perfect environment.

There's also a spicy back-and-forth when Vlad puts an old Greg Maxwell forum post on screen. Alex is not impressed by the drama angle: "I like Paul and I like even Sergio," he says, he just cares about the data. They agree to keep arguing when they're 80.

**Lore Drop 📜:** in 2009, nodes found each other through an IRC channel and announced themselves with a username that was their IP and port encoded. So an old crash log is basically a guest list of who was on Bitcoin that night. Wild.
